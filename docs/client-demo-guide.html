Fourty Distribution OS

This guide is written for live demos. It explains, in plain language, what Fourty is for, what each screen does, what people should do there day to day, and what each role is allowed to touch. Use it as your talking script while you click through the product.

Owner · Storekeeper · Subagent Navy + coral product language Default money: ETB

Who uses Fourty

Three kinds of people share one system. Their menus look different on purpose, so each person only sees the work that belongs to them.

Owner

Runs the company

The owner is the person who sets up the business in Fourty and keeps an eye on every store. They create the product list, open new shops, invite staff, and review money coming back from the field. When something looks wrong — a late closeout, a remittance that needs checking, unusual stock movement — this is the account that investigates.

They should: set policies once, review exceptions daily, and use Reports / Audit for decisions.

Storekeeper

Runs one store

The storekeeper lives inside a single location. They receive stock, sell across the counter, fix count mistakes, close the till at the end of the day, and hand cartons to subagents when the owner authorizes field distribution. They never manage the whole company catalog or other people’s logins.

They should: keep inventory truthful, record every sale with proof, and submit closeouts on time.

Subagent

Sells in the field

A subagent does not own store inventory. They receive a batch of cartons, sell from that batch, upload proof when they sell, and send money back as remittances. Their home base in the app is “My batches,” sales, remittances, notifications, and settings.

They should: only sell from active batches, remit regularly, and watch for overdue batch warnings.

Signing in only proves who you are. What you can do afterward is controlled by the menu you see, soft “access denied” screens on some pages, and database rules that hide other people’s data.

A day in the business (story to tell the client)

Use this narrative before you dive into screens. It makes the product feel like their real operation.

Morning starts with stock. The storekeeper (or owner) opens Restock when a delivery arrives and puts cartons onto the shelf in Fourty — not only in the physical storeroom. Throughout the day, counter sales are logged under Sales with a screenshot as proof. If a field seller needs product, someone issues a batch from Subagents. That person sells from the batch, then sends money through Remittances. At closing time the storekeeper submits a Daily closeout with cash declared. The owner reviews remittances and closeouts, then checks Reports to see whether the day was healthy.

Everything important leaves a trail: sales, stock moves, account changes, and reviews show up in notifications and, for owners, in the audit log. That is how Fourty turns a cigarette distribution business from “notebooks and phone calls” into a shared, accountable system.

Login

/login · Everyone enters here

The login screen is the front door. There is no public self-signup — accounts are created by the owner on the Team page, then handed to staff. That keeps the tenant clean and prevents random people from joining the distribution network.

On larger screens the page feels branded: a full background, the Fourty mark, and a clear sign-in form. On phones it collapses into a focused card so people can log in quickly in the field.

What people should do here

  • Use the email and temporary password the owner gave them.
  • After first login, go to Settings → Security and change the password.
  • If they land on the wrong page after login, the app still remembers redirects.

Dashboard

/app/dashboard · “How is the business doing right now?”

Dashboard is the morning briefing. It answers the questions owners and storekeepers ask before they dig into detail: How much did we sell? How many cartons moved? Are we running low? How many field batches are still open? How many stores and subagents are active?

Below the numbers sit charts: revenue versus cartons over time, brand mix, brand performance, a store comparison view, and a simple stock-health readout. The “By store” table lets you drill into a location. Filters at the top change the story — today, this week, this month, or a custom range. Owners can also switch stores.

What people should do here

  • Owners: start every review meeting on Dashboard. Pick the period, glance at low stock and pending batches, then jump to the page that needs action.
  • Storekeepers: confirm their store’s day is tracking — especially cartons and low-stock warnings — before they open Inventory or Restock.
  • Subagents: use it as a lightweight snapshot; their real work still lives in Sales and My batches.
  • Export the trend when someone asks for a quick Excel snapshot.

Inventory

/app/inventory · Truth about what is on the shelf

Inventory is where Fourty shows carton counts against the minimum each store should keep. If Restock and Sales are recorded correctly, this page becomes the living stock ledger — not a spreadsheet that goes stale by afternoon.

You can filter to low or healthy lines, search by brand or SKU, and open a row for details. Stock-level bars give a quick visual of which products are fine and which are under pressure. Owners and storekeepers can set the minimum for a line; owners also get a bulk “set min stock” tool when they want one policy across stores.

What people should do here

  • Check low-stock filters every morning and restock before shelves empty.
  • Set realistic minimums — high enough to avoid stockouts, low enough not to over-order.
  • If a count is wrong in real life, fix it with Adjustments, not by editing history by hand.
  • Export when auditors or accountants want a snapshot.
Subagents do not use Inventory. Their “stock” is the batch they were issued.

Sales

/app/sales · Every recorded sale, with proof

Sales is the operational history of money made. Each row is a transaction: which brand, how many cartons, how much was charged, who sold it, whether it was a store counter sale or a subagent field sale, and whether a screenshot was attached.

Charts on the page show daily volume and the mix between store and subagent channels. Filters let you narrow by period, brand, and channel. Opening a row shows the full detail; tapping a proof thumbnail opens a large viewer so managers can verify the sale actually happened.

What people should do here

  • Use New sale for every real transaction — do not “catch up” from memory days later if you can avoid it.
  • Owners: spot-check proofs during the week, especially high-value tickets.
  • Filter to the subagent channel when reconciling field performance.
  • Export when you need a clean list for finance.

New sale

/app/sales/new · Record the sale while it is still fresh

This is the most important daily action in Fourty. A sale here is not only a money record — it also reduces inventory (for store sales) or batch hand-stock (for subagents). That is why skipping this screen creates every downstream problem: fake stock, wrong closeouts, and messy remittance reviews.

At the store counter

Owners pick any store; storekeepers are locked to theirs. They choose a brand, see how many cartons are available, enter quantity and unit price, add optional notes, and attach a screenshot. The total updates live so the seller can confirm the ticket before saving.

In the field (subagent)

Subagents do not browse the whole warehouse. They pick an active batch that still has cartons in hand. Store and brand come from that batch automatically. When they save, Fourty deducts from the batch — not from general store inventory.

Organization settings can require screenshots on every sale. When that policy is on, the form will not accept a sale without proof — which is exactly what you want for accountability demos.

What people should do here

  • Capture the proof photo immediately (receipt, handover, or agreed evidence).
  • Double-check quantity against what physically left the shelf or bag.
  • Subagents: never invent sales without a batch — if no batch is active, ask for a new issue.
  • After save, confirm the sale appears in Sales history.

Restock

/app/restock · When cartons arrive, put them into Fourty

Restock is the inbound gate. Deliveries, warehouse transfers into a shop, or any “stock came in” event should be recorded here with quantity and unit cost. That keeps inventory value honest and gives later reports something real to calculate from.

The page has two halves: a simple form to add stock, and a history of past restock events you can search and export. Unit cost often defaults from the brand’s cost price so people are not guessing every time.

What people should do here

  • Log restocks the same day the delivery lands — same day, same count.
  • Use notes for invoice numbers or supplier references.
  • If stock went out for damage or correction, that belongs on Adjustments, not Restock.

Daily closeout

/app/closeout · End-of-day cash declaration

Closeout is the store’s daily handshake with management. The storekeeper declares how much cash / transfer they are holding for the day, adds opening and closing notes if needed, and submits. Fourty also surfaces today’s sales context for the selected store so the declaration is not made in the dark.

Submitted closeouts wait for owner review. That review step is how the business confirms “the till matches what the system thinks we sold.” History keeps statuses visible: submitted, reviewed, and any drafts you still use operationally.

What people should do here

  • Storekeepers: submit every working day, even if the note is short. Silence creates suspicion.
  • Owners: review submitted closeouts promptly and ask questions in notes when cash and sales disagree.
  • Use the store filter (owners) when you supervise multiple locations.
Subagents are soft-denied on this page. Field cash returns through Remittances, not store closeouts.

Stock adjustments

/app/adjustments · Fix reality when counts drift

No warehouse stays perfect. Cartons get damaged, counts drift, product returns to a supplier, or someone finds an extra sleeve during stock-take. Adjustments are the honest way to move inventory up or down with a reason — instead of quietly editing numbers with no explanation.

Reasons include count correction, damage, shrinkage, return to supplier, and other. Always leave a note when the story is not obvious. History is searchable and exportable so owners can audit who changed what.

What people should do here

  • Prefer small, frequent corrections over giant mystery dumps at month-end.
  • Use negative quantities to remove stock, positive to add found stock.
  • Never use adjustments to “fake” a sale — sales belong on the Sales page.

Remittances

/app/remittances · Sending money back to the business

Remittances are how cash from the field (and sometimes from stores) is formally declared and reviewed. A user submits an amount, payment method (bank transfer, mobile money, or cash deposit), a reference, optional proof (image or PDF), and notes. Subagent submissions are tagged so reviewers know who sent them.

Pending items wait for confirmation or rejection by an owner or storekeeper. That two-step flow is deliberate: anyone can claim they paid; only reviewers mark it as real in the system.

What people should do here

  • Subagents: remit on a rhythm (daily or after each significant sales day). Attach proof whenever you can.
  • Reviewers: confirm only when the reference / proof matches the bank or till. Reject with a reason when it does not.
  • Watch the Pending KPI — a growing queue means money is moving outside the system’s visibility.

Stores

/app/stores · The map of locations (owner only)

Before anyone can restock or sell at a shop, that shop must exist here. Owners create stores with a name, short code, address, city, phone, and active flag. Deactivating a store does not erase history — it simply stops using that location going forward.

What owners should do here

  • Create a store before creating storekeeper accounts for that location.
  • Use clear codes (for example BOLE-01) so reports stay readable.
  • Deactivate closed shops instead of deleting — keep the paper trail.

Brands

/app/brands · The product catalog (owner only)

Brands are the sellable products — typically cigarette lines with SKU, carton size, selling price, and cost. Sales, restocks, batches, and inventory all hang off this catalog. If a brand is wrong here, every downstream number inherits the mistake.

What owners should do here

  • Set carton size and prices carefully before go-live.
  • Deactivate discontinued products instead of removing them mid-history.
  • Update cost prices when supplier costs change so restock defaults stay useful.

Subagents / My batches

/app/subagents · Handing stock to the field and tracking it

This page is the bridge between store inventory and street selling. Owners and storekeepers issue batches: pick a store, a subagent, a brand, and a quantity. Those cartons become “in hand” for the subagent. As they sell, sold rises and in-hand falls. Leftovers can be returned to the store. Batches that sit open too long get overdue / stale signals so nobody “forgets” product in the field.

For the subagent, the same route is labeled My batches. They do not issue stock or manage the roster — they simply see their own ledger and act on it.

What people should do here

  • Issuers: only issue what the person can sell soon. Big idle batches create risk.
  • Issuers: return leftovers promptly when a campaign or week ends.
  • Subagents: check in-hand before promising customers product.
  • Use status filters to find overdue batches during weekly reviews.

Team

/app/users · Creating and managing people (owner only)

Team is where the owner builds the workforce inside Fourty. Creating an account asks for name, email, phone, role, store (required for storekeepers and subagents), and a password — with a generator if you want a strong temporary one. After create, Fourty shows the credentials once so you can copy them to the person securely.

Later you can change role, store, or active status. You cannot deactivate yourself — that safety check avoids locking the last owner out by accident.

What owners should do here

  • Create stores and brands first, then people.
  • Hand credentials privately; ask staff to change password on first login.
  • Deactivate leavers quickly so old phones stop receiving access.
Non-owners who open this URL see a clear soft-deny. The create API also rejects non-owners.

Reports

/app/reports · Deep dive for owners and storekeepers

If Dashboard is the briefing, Reports is the investigation. It layers KPIs, daily breakdowns, channel mix, top sellers, proof coverage, store comparison, brand mix, a seller leaderboard, a screenshot gallery, and a transaction table you can search. Export produces Excel with summary and sales detail for offline analysis.

Owners can look across the company; storekeepers stay inside their store. Subagents are soft-denied — their job is selling and remitting, not company analytics.

What people should do here

  • Use proof coverage to see whether screenshot discipline is slipping.
  • Compare stores when deciding where to send inventory next.
  • Open the gallery when training staff on what “good proof” looks like.
  • Export before monthly business reviews.

Audit trail

/app/audit · Who did what, when (owner only)

The audit trail is the black box recorder. Important actions — settings changes, sensitive updates, operational events the system chooses to log — appear with actor, role, action, entity, store, timestamp, and detail payload. Owners can filter, open full JSON detail, and export. Nobody edits the log; that is the point.

What owners should do here

  • Check audit after any dispute (“who changed that price / role / setting?”).
  • Filter by action type when investigating a narrow incident.
  • Export around month-end if compliance needs a freeze of activity.

Notifications

/app/notifications · The in-app inbox for everyone

When something needs attention — a closeout submitted, a remittance waiting, low stock, system notices — Fourty writes an in-app notification. The sidebar badge shows unread count so people do not have to hunt. Opening an item can mark it read and, when a link is present, jump straight to the related screen.

What people should do here

  • Clear unread items daily; a noisy badge trains people to ignore alerts.
  • Use “Open link” instead of searching for the record manually.
  • Pair this with push alerts (Settings) if they need to know when away from the desk.

Settings

/app/settings · Personal setup, alerts, and company rules

Settings is split into tabs so each concern stays clear. Everyone manages their own profile, password, notification preferences, and theme. Only owners see Organization, where company-wide policies live.

Profile

Update display name and phone. Email, role, and store are shown but not casually editable here — role and store changes go through Team (owner).

Security

Change password with current + new + confirm (minimum length enforced). Sign out of the current session when using a shared device.

Notifications (push)

This tab turns browser push on for the current device, lists registered devices, lets you send a test notification, and controls which event types may push. Quiet hours silence push during sleep time while still saving in-app notifications.

On iPhone, Safari tabs cannot receive web push. The person must Add Fourty to the Home Screen and open it from that icon, then enable alerts. Android Chrome works in a normal browser tab over HTTPS.

Appearance

Choose Light, Dark, or System so the UI matches how they already use their phone.

Organization (owner only)

Company name and contact, currency, timezone, default minimum stock, closeout reminder hour, fiscal year start, and policy switches:

  • Require sale screenshots — forces proof on new sales.
  • Require remittance proof — forces evidence when sending money.
  • Allow negative store stock — usually leave off unless you have a special process.

About

Short product reminder plus the user’s role, currency, and timezone — useful during demos when someone asks “what am I looking at?”

What people should do here

  • Everyone: enable push on their main phone, then tap Send test once.
  • Everyone: set quiet hours so overnight alerts do not burn trust.
  • Owners: decide screenshot / proof policies before go-live training.
  • Use Export my data if someone wants a personal JSON backup of profile and preferences.

Patterns you will see everywhere

Call these out once so later screens feel familiar instead of new.

Working on phones

Tables become card lists on small screens. Filters sit in a compact row you can scroll. Row taps open detail dialogs. The shell keeps a sidebar trigger, theme toggle, and sign-out within reach.

Proof and exports

Sales want screenshots; remittances can attach image or PDF. ProofViewer opens large previews. Most operational pages export Excel when someone needs a file outside the app.

Soft-deny pages

Some roles can type a URL they should not use. Instead of a broken page, Fourty shows a calm access message. That is better for demos than a crash — and it teaches the boundary without drama.

Suggested live demo script (15–20 minutes)

Follow this order so the story builds instead of jumping randomly.

  1. Login as owner — show the branded gate, then land on Dashboard.
  2. Dashboard — narrate KPIs and filters; point at low stock / pending batches.
  3. Stores & Brands — “this is the skeleton of the business.”
  4. Team — create or show a storekeeper and subagent; explain one-time credentials.
  5. Restock — put cartons into a store; open Inventory to prove the count moved.
  6. Issue a batch — hand stock to a subagent; show in-hand.
  7. New sale (store) — attach a screenshot; show it in Sales.
  8. Switch to subagent — sell from the batch; show batch numbers change.
  9. Remittance — submit as subagent; confirm as owner/storekeeper.
  10. Closeout — submit as storekeeper; review as owner.
  11. Reports + Audit — “this is how you manage by evidence.”
  12. Settings — org policies + push test; mention iPhone Home Screen rule.
